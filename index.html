🎮 Game development for beginners

Level Up: Making Your First Game

Game development is the craft of turning an idea into something people can play. It mixes art, code, sound and design, but you don't need to master all of them to begin. Start with something tiny, finish it, and every game after that gets easier.

How a Game Gets Made

Most games, big or small, go through the same five stages.

  1. 💡

    Idea

    Pick one simple thing the player does, like jump, match or dodge, and build around it.

  2. 🧪

    Prototype

    Make a rough, ugly version fast to find out whether the idea is actually fun.

  3. 🎨

    Art & Sound

    Add visuals, music and sound effects that make the game feel alive.

  4. 🕹️

    Playtest

    Watch other people play without helping them, then fix what confuses them.

  5. 🚀

    Release

    Share it with friends or online. A finished small game beats an unfinished big one.

Beginner Projects & Game Jams

Small projects that teach the basics, plus popular game jams to join. Use the buttons to filter by type.

  • 🏓

    Paddle & Ball

    Two paddles, one ball. A classic first project for learning movement, collisions and scoring.

    • 2D
  • 🗺️

    Text Adventure

    Rooms, items and typed commands. Teaches game logic without needing any art.

    • Text
  • 🍄

    One-Screen Platformer

    Run, jump and reach the flag. Good practice for gravity, controls and level design.

    • 2D
  • 🎱

    Rolling-Ball Maze

    Tilt a board to guide a ball to the goal. A gentle first step into 3D physics and cameras.

    • 3D
  • 🏃

    Endless Runner

    Dodge obstacles that speed up over time. Teaches spawning, difficulty curves and high scores.

    • 2D
  • 📖

    Choose-Your-Path Story

    A short branching story with a few endings. Great for writers who want to try game design.

    • Text
  • 🏠

    Tiny 3D Room

    Build one cozy room the player can walk around and explore. Mood matters more than mechanics.

    • 3D
  • 🔥

    Brackeys Game Jam

    A week-long online jam held twice a year on itch.io. The extra time makes it a relaxed first jam.

    • Jam
  • ⏱️

    GMTK Game Jam

    A 48-hour summer jam hosted by Game Maker's Toolkit, and one of the biggest on itch.io. Keep your idea tiny.

    • Jam
  • 🏆

    Ludum Dare

    One of the oldest online jams, running since 2002. Try the 72-hour Jam with friends or the solo 48-hour Compo.

    • Jam
  • 🌍

    Global Game Jam

    A yearly 48-hour jam at local sites and online around the world. A great way to meet other developers.

    • Jam
